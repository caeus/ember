**Logline.** Hugo clears the flat ahead of a deadline, is briefly pleased that the units are
still running, and unplugs them because you unplug things when you move.

**Beats**

1. The deadline, and nothing more than a deadline. A letter, a reasonable notice period he
   has already spent. No confrontation, nobody cruel — he stopped paying rent months ago
   because he was somewhere else, and the world has responded proportionately.
   **The notice is shown. It says: vacate by 26 October 2031.** No commentary, no character
   noticing, no line after it that knows. The reader has spent the whole book with that
   date and meets it here as boilerplate on a form.
2. The flat as he finds it: unlived in. Months of mail. The evidence of a person with
   hobbies and plans, left mid-sentence. He has been sleeping in a chair in a ward.
3. Packing is sorting — keep, bin, don't decide. He is fast and unsentimental, not from
   strength but because he is too tired to be sentimental.
4. The units, under the desk, still on. Warm, furred with dust, LEDs doing what they've
   been doing. **He is briefly, mildly pleased.** Three months of uptime, no crash, no
   thermal fault. A small technical satisfaction, the only pleasant thing in the scene.
   `[This is the payload. On a reread it is three months of a person under.]`
5. He handles them — moves them, coils cable, repowers one to check something, sets them
   down somewhere else. `[Per canon §4, this handling is the first replicas. He notices
   nothing and the prose reports nothing. The reader cannot be given this.]`
6. He unplugs them. No weight, no line break, no pause before it. You unplug things when
   you move.
7. Something registers faintly — the world a little more alive and a little more dull at
   once. He files it under exhaustion, or grief, and he is not wrong enough about that to
   look again.
8. **The units go in the box.** He takes them with him. End on something logistical: keys,
   a last door, a time.

**Notes**

- **The scene never leaves the flat.** No cutaway, no elsewhere, no final line that knows
  what just happened in forty wards. Placed after row 8, the reader already knows the cohort
  woke, and supplies the detonation unaided.
- **The external factor is the deadline, not a person.** Nobody in this scene opposes him,
  threatens him, or takes anything from him. His hand does the unplugging; the decision was
  made by a date he didn't set. Canon §7's rule survives — he is the instrument of the turn,
  not its author — and the book stays kinder than a confrontation would make it.
- **Beat 4 is the whole scene.** The one moment of satisfaction in three months of bedside
  vigil is pride in the thing that caused it. It must be written small and genuinely
  pleasant. Any shading toward dread cashes the scene early.
- **Beat 1 carries the reveal** (canon §6a). It only works if the book has been sparing
  with explicit dates everywhere else, so the vacate-by line is one of very few printed
  dates in the novel. That is a constraint on every other scene, not on this one.
- **Beat 8 is why there is a book.** He packs them. He could have binned them with the rest
  of the dead electronics and been an innocent man for the rest of his life.
- No suspicion here, and nothing resembling a theory (canon §7). The correlation only
  becomes available to him afterwards, when the waking is in the news and the date is one he
  can match.
- Pairs against G007: there he watches one person intently and concludes nothing. Here he
  isn't watching anything and causes everything.

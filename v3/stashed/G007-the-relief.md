**Logline.** Hugo runs it a second time, watches one person, and is happy — while the
report that reaches him later is about other people.

**Beats**

1. Establish the relation first and without sentiment. `[OPEN: who]` Someone whose bed he
   has sat beside, whose bad news he is the contact for. Not an abstraction and not a
   beloved — a person he is **administratively** responsible for, which is more intimate
   than love in the ways that matter here.
2. He runs it. The setup is still improvised — this is not yet G006's checklist. He has
   convinced himself the run is a formality, and the care he takes contradicts that.
3. **He watches one thing only.** Not the news, not a sample, not the phenomenon. Them.
   The scene's whole field of attention is one person, which is exactly why his conclusions
   will be worthless and why he will believe them.
4. Nothing happens to them.
5. **The relief.** Physical, total, unearned. Write it as genuine happiness — he is not
   suppressing anything, there is nothing in him being overridden. The horror is that
   there's nothing to override.
6. The conclusion he draws from a sample of one: coincidence. `[This is the hinge of the
   entire book. Every later run proceeds from this sentence, and the scene must make it
   *reasonable* — a frightened person reaching the conclusion that lets him stop being
   frightened.]`
7. Only afterwards, incidentally, the rest: a report, a number, people elsewhere. He reads
   it the way anyone reads about strangers. **He does not connect it to beat 5**, and the
   prose must not connect it for him either — the reader does that work alone.
8. End small. Something ordinary he does next, having decided he is not responsible.

**Notes**

- **This scene is why there are four runs.** Without it, Hugo's repetition reads as
  recklessness. With it, it reads as a man who tested the only hypothesis he could bear and
  got the answer he wanted from a sample size of one.
- **It is also why he connected anything in the first place.** Nobody else had grounds to
  correlate a private hobby with a stranger's medical event. He had grounds to correlate it
  with *theirs*, because he was already watching that bed. His guilt is downstream of his
  attention being personal. `[Worth considering: he is the only person in the world with
  both halves of the correlation, and he got them by accident.]`
- **Pairs with G001.** There, a caretaker gives a stranger everything because answering is
  the only useful thing left to her. Here, that stranger is doing this with what she gave
  him. Reading them in either order should damage both.
- POV discipline: no theory (canon §7). He concludes *coincidence*, which is a verdict, not
  a model.
- `[OPEN — the chronology this scene depends on]` Whether the related sleeper is under or
  awake during run 2 changes what beat 4 means: either they fail to be taken *again*, or
  they fail to be taken *at all*. See the disconnection question in `ledger.md`. The beats
  above are written to survive both, but a draft cannot be.
- Do **not** write beat 5 as foreshadowing. If the sentence knows what's coming, the scene
  is a confession instead of a trap.

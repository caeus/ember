**Logline.** Hugo sets up the fourth run with a checklist, varies one parameter because
good method requires it, and does not use the word coincidence any more.

**Beats**

1. The setup is **fast now**. Three runs of practice. He has a checklist, a known order, a
   cable he keeps because it's the one that works. The competence is the horror and the
   prose should not point at it.
2. The notebook. **Run 1 is a reconstruction** — he fired it by accident and logged it
   months afterwards from memory, so its entry is thinner than the others, hedged, with
   gaps where a number should be. Runs 2 and 3 are properly logged, conditions and timings,
   something varied each time. The reader sees the entries differ without being told why
   that matters. `[Payload: this is the variance an entire institutional effort is breaking
   itself against. Do not let him note that.]`
3. **The gaps in run 1 are the engine.** He cannot reproduce conditions he never recorded,
   so he cannot rule himself out, so he runs it again. His exoneration requires data he
   destroyed by not taking it. Everything after run 1 is an attempt to recover an accident.
4. He no longer tells himself it's coincidence. He has newer words — *bounding* it,
   *characterizing* it, being *responsible about it*. The vocabulary of a man performing
   science as penance.
5. He changes one parameter, deliberately, because method demands variation. The scene must
   let the reader feel that changing a number means changing what happens to strangers,
   **without Hugo articulating it** (canon §7).
6. How he learns outcomes: not instruments — reports. `[PROPOSED in canon §4]` He checks
   what came of run 3 the same way everyone else does, scrolling past it among other news.
   He has become a man who reads his results in strangers' lives.
7. **Stopping is available.** He considers it in flat practical terms — what he'd do with
   the units, what he'd tell himself afterwards — and declines, for a reason that is not
   about knowledge. `[OPEN: what the reason is. It should not be curiosity and it should
   not be malice.]`
8. He starts it. End on something small and procedural — a checklist item ticked, a lid
   closed, the time noted.

**Notes**

- **This is the scene the recursion needs.** The premise — he causes events by trying to
  prove he doesn't — is grotesque in summary and easy to flatten into villainy. It only
  works if the reader watches him be *reasonable*. Every beat should be defensible from
  inside his head and indefensible from outside it.
- Against G004: there, the second run, he catches himself hoping. Here the hoping is
  settled and the method has absorbed it. **If G004 and G006 both survive, they must not be
  the same scene twice** — G004 is a man arguing with himself, G006 is a man who has
  finished arguing.
- MYSTERY: the reader gets everything and still cannot predict the next event, because
  Hugo's parameters mean nothing to them either.
- Withheld: the outcome of run 4. It belongs to someone who never learns a cause.
- `[OPEN]` Does he know, by run 4, that people are going under? Almost certainly. Whether
  the scene lets the reader be *sure* he knows is a separate and better question.

- **Run 1 was an accident** (your note). He did not decide to do it the first time, which
  is what makes the next three decisions and not repetitions. It also leaves the original
  event under-documented by the only person who could have documented it — see beat 3.
- Links to G005: if run 1 fired accidentally, *what fired it* is live, and G005's
  `[OPEN: does she touch it?]` is suddenly a candidate answer. **Keep both open.** The two
  scenes must not be written so that one confirms the other.

**Terminology debt.** He has no name for these. A reader needs one, the institutions need
one, and his private word is a third thing — see the naming question in `ledger.md`.

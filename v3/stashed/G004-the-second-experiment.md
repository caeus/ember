**Logline.** Hugo sets up another run to confirm the coincidence he has already decided
on, and catches himself hoping it isn't.

**Beats**

1. Hugo alone with the rig. Restate the setup in its own mundane terms — nothing in it
   refers to minds, sleep or people. `[OPEN: crypto / home automation / thesis / a game]`
   The scene should be readable as a man doing hobbyist technical work.
2. His stated reason, which he has rehearsed and which is almost true: run it again, get
   nothing, stop thinking about this.
3. The care he takes is wildly disproportionate to a null result. He logs onset conditions,
   timestamps, the neighbours' hours, what's running on the same circuit. He notices the
   disproportion himself and keeps logging.
4. A small domestic interruption establishes that he is now secretive — the notebook goes
   out of sight, the lie is ready before anyone needs it. Low stakes, no confrontation.
5. He runs it. **The waiting is the scene.**
6. In the waiting he catches himself hoping, examines the hope honestly, and finds it isn't
   curiosity. A null result would mean he is a man who briefly imagined he mattered, and he
   would rather be guilty than be that.
7. He does not stop the run. End on the ordinariness of the room, and on him deciding what
   time he'll check the news in the morning.

**Notes**

- MYSTERY without revelation: the reader learns Hugo's relationship to the thing, not the
  mechanism. Outcome is **not shown here** — it belongs to another POV entirely, ideally
  someone who never learns a cause.
- This is the genome that turns him from secretive to culpable. Everything before it can
  be read as a man failing to connect dots. Nothing after it can.
- Placement constraint: must land late enough that the reader has already lived through
  material this scene reverses. If it arrives before the audience has accumulated
  sympathy for the affected, it is only a plot beat.
- Resist any line where Hugo articulates a theory of the Field. He has observations and
  dread, not a model.

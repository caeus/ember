**Logline.** A sister who has run out of useful things to do answers a stranger's questions,
is more generous with him than she has been with any doctor, and is left with a worse
question than the one she came in with.

**Beats**

1. Ward routine, compressed. She has learned to read her brother's body like weather — the
   twitch under the eyelid, the chapped lip, the way the staff have quietly stopped using
   the word *coma* because the EEG embarrasses it.
2. A visitor signs in as a graduate student running a quality-of-care survey. Lanyard,
   clipboard, shoes slightly wrong for the story. He never names a department and she never
   asks.
3. His first questions are plausible and dull: onset date, what her brother had eaten,
   whether he'd been ill, which GP.
4. Then they converge on **time**, and stay there. What time exactly. How does she know.
   Was there a clock, a programme running, a message with a timestamp on it. He asks it four
   ways and writes the answers to the minute — far past anything a care survey could want.
5. A few that are merely strange: where exactly was he, was he indoors, had the power gone
   out that week, did anything in the flat hum. `[These are Hugo's superstitions, not
   clues. He is projecting his own room onto theirs, and the scene should read as a man
   checking something private rather than an author planting evidence.]`
6. **She was in the room when it happened.** She mentions it in passing, as context for the
   timing. He asks her to say that again.
7. She answers everything, because answering is the first useful thing anyone has let her do
   in seven weeks — including the small domestic detail she's been too embarrassed to tell
   the doctors `[OPEN: what]`.
8. He writes that one in a notebook, not on the clipboard form. She registers the difference
   without knowing what to do with registering it. A nurse asks whether he's family; he
   thanks her, doesn't finish the survey, and goes. She never got his name, and later
   notices the form was blank.
9. Back at the bedside. The question she can't put down isn't his. It's that she was there,
   in the room, at the minute it happened — **and nothing happened to her.**

**Notes**

- **Beat 6 is the scene.** A witness present at onset who was not taken is cohort
  membership made personal: why him and not her. Canon §7 says membership stays
  unexplained, so she will never get an answer, and the question is designed to be
  permanent rather than pending.
- `[PROPOSED]` **This is why Hugo chose this family.** He seeks cases with someone present
  at onset — a person who can give him a timestamp and a room. It explains the visit
  without explaining him, and it means the warmth of beats 7–8 was a selection criterion.
- No one deduces anything. The reader gets a stranger who cares a great deal about clocks;
  the caretaker gets a bad night.
- Recontextualizes hard after reveal row 8: the notebook becomes collection, not help, and
  his interest in her presence becomes an interest in why his own run missed her.
- POV discipline — Hugo is only ever *observed*. No access to why he's asking.
- Deliberately withheld: his name, his face in any memorable detail, the notebook's contents.
- Pairs with a much-later genome where she sees him again and the scene pays out.

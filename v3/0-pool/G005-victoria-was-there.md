**Logline.** Victoria spends an unremarkable evening at Hugo's, finds his equipment mildly
annoying, and leaves. He has not started yet.

**Beats**

1. Open with no signal that this matters. No "that was the last ordinary night", no weather
   doing work. The flashback's position in the book is the only emphasis it gets.
2. Victoria is here for her own reasons, on her own terms. Establish what they actually are
   to each other `[OPEN]` — and make it more ordinary than either *lovers* or *using him*.
   Something with no name and no stakes is harder to see coming and worse to look back on.
3. The rig is in the room and beneath her notice. She registers it the way anyone registers
   a router: warm, blinking, badly placed, slightly loud. `[OPEN: does she touch it?]` —
   move it off the chair, unplug something to charge her phone, complain about the heat.
   This is the largest open question in the scene and it should stay open as long as possible.
4. They talk about nothing with any bearing on anything. The scene's job is to be
   uneventful, and the draft must be disciplined enough to let it be boring in the right way.
5. `[OPEN]` whether they sleep together. If yes, the prose gives it no more weight than
   anything else in the evening — it becomes significant only in retrospect, and only if the
   priming reveal (reveal-order row 7) is live.
6. Hugo is distracted by the thing he's about to run. She reads this as him being tedious,
   or performing busyness. He states what it's for, once, flatly, in its own mundane terms.
   Neither she nor the reader retains it. That sentence is the scene's payload.
7. She leaves. He hasn't started. Her last action in the room is the hinge `[OPEN: what]`.
8. End without portent. No closing line that knows what's coming.

**Notes**

- **The book should never show him starting it.** This scene ends before, and the
  consequences have already been lived through hundreds of pages earlier. The gap is the
  point.
- **What this must not settle:** the direction of causation. Her presence may be
  coincidence, may be why it worked, may be how she became what she is, may be irrelevant.
  All four readings stay open after the last line. If the scene answers, it has failed.
- **POV decides the meaning.** Victoria's POV makes it a scene about how little it
  registered — the reveal lands through her indifference, and her agency survives. Hugo's
  makes it a scene about concealment and hands him exposition he isn't allowed (canon §7, Hugo never theorizes). A
  third party who knew them both is the coldest option and the only one where neither of
  them gets to frame it. **Recommend Victoria**, and the ID keeps that unless you say
  otherwise.
- **Planting debt.** For this to reverse rather than confirm, nothing earlier in the book
  may let the reader put them in the same room — no shared acquaintance, no near-miss. That
  constraint is now load-bearing for every Victoria and every Hugo scene preceding it.
- **The first run was an accident** (canon §4 / G006). That raises the stakes on beat 3
  enormously: if nobody decided to start it, something did. Her hand is now one of the
  available answers, alongside a timer, a fault, a cat, nothing at all. The scene must not
  settle it and must not wink at it.
- Pairs against G004: there, Hugo is meticulous about onset conditions and logs everything
  on the same circuit. He never logs *who was in the flat*.

**Logline.** A sister answers an investigator's unusually precise questions about when her brother became unresponsive, and learns that the timing which makes his case interesting cannot explain why she was spared.

**Beats**

1. Seven weeks in the ward. She has learned to read her brother's body like weather: the twitch beneath an eyelid, the chapped lip, the staff's quiet abandonment of the word *coma* because the EEG does not fit.
2. A junior clinical researcher, working with a handful of unexplained cases, asks permission to interview her. He doesn't claim to have found a cause. Some accounts place the onset of other patients' stupors within the same narrow window, although times supplied by families are approximate and hospital intake times are worse.
3. His questions are prosaic: when did her brother last speak normally, when did she first notice, was there a call or message she could date? He is interested in **onset**, not the time an ambulance arrived.
4. She says she was in the room with her brother. The researcher asks her to clarify what she actually witnessed. She remembers less than she thought: a sentence stopped in the middle, an ordinary action unfinished, the awful interval before she understood.
5. She gives him the small domestic detail she was too embarrassed to tell the doctors `[OPEN: what]`. He records it alongside her uncertainty instead of treating the detail as a clue.
6. He shows her why he is asking about the minute. A few other reports *might* coincide with it. She asks whether that means someone knows what happened. He answers that even the clocks may be wrong.
7. Their conversation finishes. For the first time in weeks, she has been useful to someone without having to pretend she is optimistic.
8. Back at the bedside, she looks at the chair in which she was sitting when it happened. If it happened to several people in the same hour, **why her brother and not her?** The investigator's table has no column for that.

**Notes**

- **The observer is not Hugo.** This is a sincere, independent attempt to understand apparent onset synchrony from sparse, imperfect records. There is no hidden technical trail to the rig, no invented solution to cohort membership, and no reason to connect the researcher to Hugo later.
- **The scene is her unanswered question.** A shared time tells people *when*, not *why these people*. See `canon.md` §§1, 3 and 7.
- The preliminary pattern may justify asking questions; it does **not** give the researcher a mechanism, make the original event fully classified, or substitute for later institutional acknowledgment.
- The family's generosity is not an investigative breakthrough. A stranger has finally treated the caretaker's recollection as worth hearing, even though it cannot give her what she needs.
- No fixed name, institution, precise times, or geographic distribution is assumed. These remain open.

Signaling and Systems was a class that Hugo loved, except for the fact that it was every monday at 7am. It was a bigger inconvenience given the teacher was obsessed with punctuality and would always make a quick 5 minute quiz at exactly the hour the class started.
These Quizzes could influence the final grade of the subject if mathematically necessary, which was Hugo's case. Class was filled with complex mathematical concepts, but certain more abstract concepts lingered behind all that formal theory.

Those concepts hinted on the nature of Ember, in a way Hugo couldn't fully explain, and so he decided to talk to Gale.

"Come on, so let's check at your notes" he said without looking at Hugo, while accessing Hugo's scorecard.

"Professor, I'm not here to talk about that" Hugo replied as he took the seat at the front of the teacher's desk.

Gale's eyes fixated on Hugo, subtly hinting surprise. Annoyance and curiosity, more the latter than the former. Ember perceived.

"Well, maybe you should, your grades don't seem very promising"

Ember started emitting a soothing hum, directed at Gale. It was one of the first attempts to influence someone else's behavior directly. Hugo was hopeful as the weird soundless harmony of emotional prompts, washed through him too, and successfully soothed his mind.

Hugo took a deep breath, steadying himself as he faced Gale's skeptical gaze. "I know, but indulge me with this professor, I’ll just explain it from the start," he said, keeping his tone casual. "It’s about a project I’ve been working on outside of class. I’ve got this programmable unit I was tinkering with—just a simple setup, really. It was supposed to be for my crypto class. Yet I left it unsupervised, isolated from any peripherals, running a looping code that behaves a little bit like a... hive? Do you know of Conway's game of life? like that sort of"

Gale’s expression shifted slightly, betraying mild curiosity. "And?"

"And… well, a few days after leaving it running, I started having strange dreams. Vivid ones," Hugo continued, glancing at the wall to avoid Gale’s piercing gaze. "At first, I thought they were stress dreams—figured maybe it was just lingering stuff from the breakup with my ex."

As he mentioned her, A sudden, inconvenient rush of lust surged through him, making his thoughts jittery, a wave of emotions not his own filtering in through Ember’s constant background eavesdropping. He knew immediately that Victoria was feeling restless, impulsive… and, by the feeling, likely close to doing something reckless. The surge of emotion made his chest tighten uncomfortably, but Hugo quickly closed off his mind to Ember’s connection, pushing the intrusive thoughts aside.

Gale raised an eyebrow, clearly uninterested in personal drama. "A breakup, huh? Not exactly groundbreaking, but I suppose emotional stress can affect one's cognitive functions."

"Anyway," Hugo resumed, his voice steady again, "the dreams didn’t stop, and they started getting… feeverish, and affecting me". Hugo's gaze now queried Gale's trying to deduce if he knew about his incident at the end of the previous semester, but found nothing. "until I turned the unit off".

Ember could perceive Gale's fading interest. "Why assume it was the unit, it could have been just coincidence"

"Well, professor, I asked a friend of mine to turn it on or off randomly once every day, and the intense dreams matched the days he turned it on". Gale's curiosiry visibly ignited after Hugo's lie.

"Ok, can you bring the unit and show me the code you printed in it?", Gale's curiosity flared with ambition end excitement.

"I broke it, professor" Hugo avoided Gale's gaze as he lied. "stupid accident. I'm actually trying to replicate it, and honestly just trying to understand if I'm just crazy, or if that's actually possible"

Gale raised an eyebrow, his disappointment overtaking his facial expression. "Broke, did it?" he asked, his tone dry. "And now you’re hoping to replicate it?"

Hugo nodded, carefully hiding his nervousness. "Yeah, pretty much."

For a moment, Gale simply studied him, the silence heavy between them. Then, slowly, he leaned back, crossing his arms. "Well, if you’re fishing for ideas, I’ll tell you this much: what you’re describing goes far beyond the scope of Signaling and Systems. There’s a concept, a rather fringe one, called ‘spontaneous synchronicity.’ A few academics like to toy with it as a thought experiment."

"Spontaneous synchronicity?" Hugo echoed, feeling a small pulse of encouragement from Ember as if to stay on this thread.

Gale nodded, his tone adopting the cadence of a teacher as he explained. "The theory proposes that certain systems, even ones that aren’t directly connected, could fall into sync if their signals or oscillations match in some way. But here’s the catch—it’s about as speculative as trying to prove gravity is a wave, or that a teacup orbits the sun." He scoffed slightly, amused by the idea. "You’d need to assume that brains emit some kind of ambient noise—like a low-grade hum—and that, on an improbable level, they might detect each other’s signals."

Hugo felt Ember’s subtle attentiveness as Gale spoke, absorbing each word. "So you’re saying it’s not impossible, just… unlikely?" he asked, trying to keep his voice casual.

"Let’s just say, Hugo, that there are a thousand barriers to it being possible," Gale replied. "If there’s any truth to it, it would be buried under layers of interference, filtering, and noise. Without any direct link—no physical connection—what you’re describing is as improbable as… well, that teacup orbiting the sun."

He smirked, clearly entertained by the absurdity of it all. But behind his skepticism, Hugo sensed a flicker of genuine intrigue, a quiet desire to see just how far this line of thought could go if only out of intellectual curiosity.

Hugo nodded, satisfied with the response for now. "Thanks, Professor. I’ll keep at it—at least until I can get it to fail less spectacularly."

"Good luck with that," Gale replied, the skepticism in his tone softened by a faint, reluctant hint of interest.

Clement watches the steam spiral off his cheap coffee, the cup sweating onto a library step. A Friday night he isn’t invited to. Couples drift past in clusters of laughter and perfume, sunlight-bronze wrists brushing, headphones shared, shoulders touching. He clocks every pairing like a test he didn’t know was scheduled. He tells himself he’s just resting, that the café was too loud, that studying outside is efficient. The truth is he has nowhere else to be.

He scrolls his usual feeds on his phone with his thumb, half-reading the same threads about “female hypergamy” he’s seen a dozen times. The words are familiar aspirin: it’s not that he’s awkward, it’s that they have bad taste. If that’s true, he thinks, then why does it still ache when a girl in a denim jacket laughs at something her boyfriend whispers and leans into his chest?

He looks away, focuses on the condensation ring his cup leaves on the concrete, a tiny proof that he exists in the same world. He adjusts his hoodie, chewed drawstrings at his chin, and tries to look like he belongs there, like he chose this.

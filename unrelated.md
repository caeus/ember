Quantum mechanics are very alien to us, but they're all around. They're however, unperceivable at our level of perception. We see light waves. And the smaller wavelength we can see (violet) is 380 nanometers, which is vastly bigger than an electron. I'm talking hundreds of millions times larger than an electron. Anyway, electrons, and other fundamental particles (and even atoms), are subjected more clearly to quantum mechanics, and... ok, closing to the point, I'm nerddumping a lot. Down there, fundamental particles state is a composition of multiple possible states. Like the Schrodinger cat. These particles can be here and there at the same time.
Quantum computing builds on top of that (a phenomenon called superposition). Anyway... anytime we want to get the result from a quantum composition, we will destroy superposition and end up with 1 of the possible states of the particles. That's collapsing, and I'm actually writing about how important is to go into interviews and literally collapse your superposition of ideas and edge cases, and possibilities and stick to one. And move on.
Otherwise you'll just end up being overwhelmed by the exponentially bigger amount of possibilities you have to deal with. Collapse! Destroy superposition. Do it smoothly, ask the interviewer for confirmation that you're going in the right direction, and ignore other possibilities. Collapse!
